# Archival acquisition record

Research date: 27 October 2026. Fourteen unique historical photographs were downloaded from Wikimedia Commons and pinned by SHA-256. `asset-manifest.json` contains captions, dates, creators or archive names, original source URLs, media URLs, local filenames, licenses, conditions and source-record evidence for each. `scripts/acquire_assets.py` accepts only cleared records and checks the content hash before use. None is AI-generated imagery or a synthetic reenactment.

The available material documents Baghdad streets (1966 and June1977), a Baghdad streetscape at the Umm al-Tubul mosque (1976), Michel Aflaq (1963), arts education and music recording (1972–1979), a private Baghdad home (1980s), the Baghdad Arab Summit (1978), and Tariq Aziz visiting the White House (1984). One image documents Iraqi university students physically in Jena in1960; its caption must retain that place and date. It predates Ba'athist government and cannot substantiate later Iraqi educational access. Specific arts-institute photographs likewise cannot establish universal educational attainment. The private-home photograph cannot stand as evidence of professional employment or universal freedom.

All fourteen files were visually inspected in a contact sheet. They appear to be genuine historical photographs consistent with the source descriptions. This inspection is not a claim to identify unknown photographers or establish every person independently. Dates are historical catalog captions, not modern scan or upload timestamps. The two1977 Baghdad photographs are only352×236 pixels, so their limited original detail remains an unavoidable quality constraint.

Licensing follows explicit source declarations: PD-self by photographer Bobby Zlatevski, CC-BY2 by wilford peloquin (Flickr review preserved), CC-BY-SA3 from Fortepan, CC0 from Nationaal Archief and the Farid Allawerdi Music Archives records, CC-BY-SA3 Germany from Bundesarchiv, and public-domain US-government photographs from the CIA and White House Photographic Office/NARA. The archive-sourced CC0 Allawerdi photographs have unidentified photographers; this fact is retained. The license declaration is preserved as source evidence; it must not be represented as an independent signed rights-transfer document.

Fortepan and Bundesarchiv assets require attribution, source/license links, identification of transformations, and a compatible share-alike license for adaptations. The rendered compilation must honor those obligations. Do not advertise the entire archival collection as CC0.

## Coverage gaps

No cleared visual was located for the actual June1972 nationalization announcement, Iraqi oilfields/refineries in the requested period, hospitals, women working in courts/hospitals/military, Baghdad's Tigris in the requested period, or general Iraqi industrial development. These remain explicit gaps, not permission to substitute modern material or unrelated photographs as evidence.

Internet Archive searches were run for Iraq movies dated1960–1989 and for Baghdad movies with licensing metadata. Search and relevant metadata snapshots are saved alongside this report. The1984 TVOntario/Britannica documentary *The Middle East: Revolution — Iraq and Iran* is access restricted and lacks a reuse license; it was not downloaded. A community upload of Iranian state bombing footage asserts CC0 without established uploader authority and was rejected. Self-licensed Iraqi tanker scans have inconsistent2015 versus historical dates and were rejected. A circa1980 Mennonite Church USA Archives loom photograph has a Flickr Commons 'No Known Copyright Restrictions' statement; it was held out because that is not an affirmative license or conclusive public-domain finding.

An additional institutional search found National Library of Israel photographs by Yosi Rot of Kurdish people in Iraq in1976, under CC-BY4, and German Federal Archive photographs of Iraqi students in East Germany. These do not fill the specific oil, medical or infrastructure gaps. They are recorded as candidates, not used as footage of Arab industrial prosperity.

Firecrawl retrieval failed because the connected account had insufficient credits. Research continued using native web search, Wikimedia's API with an identifying User-Agent, and institutional/Internet Archive metadata. An initial403 from Commons was resolved by supplying the User-Agent; it was not a persistent blocker.
