# THE RESURRECTION — narration fact-check

Checked 27 October 2026. The exact supplied narration is **not approved as an unqualified factual account**. It combines real policies/events, misleading universal outcomes, and political rhetoric. It can be presented as an attributed political remembrance only if the specific corrections below are legibly synchronized on screen and the repression context is included. These are editorial accuracy conditions, not a legal prohibition on discussing the ideology.

The submitted spoken text is retained by the production configuration; proposed replacements below are separate and must not be substituted silently. The final Arabic slogan is a party slogan, not a historical verdict.

## Findings and synchronized corrections

### oil_date

**Supplied claim:** June 1st 1972. Iraq nationalized its oil.

**Verdict:** accurate_date_overbroad_scope. 1 June nationalized IPC assets in northern Iraq; affiliated Mosul and Basrah operations were excluded from that decree.

**On-screen qualification:** 1 JUNE 1972 · IPC nationalized / Other oil holdings followed later.

**Optional revised narration:** June 1st, 1972. Iraq nationalized the Iraq Petroleum Company.

**Evidence:** S1, S13.

### bp

**Supplied claim:** The wealth extracted for fifty years by British Petroleum — came home.

**Verdict:** materially_misleading. BP was one consortium shareholder (23.75%). The 1925 concession predates 1972 by 47 years; it is not evidence of fifty years of BP extraction.

**On-screen qualification:** IPC was a multinational consortium. / BP held 23.75%; the concession dated to 1925.

**Optional revised narration:** The assets of a foreign-owned consortium, including British Petroleum, passed to the Iraqi state.

**Evidence:** S1.

### income

**Supplied claim:** Per capita income tripled.

**Verdict:** requires_metric_dates_and_price_basis. Nominal GDP/person nearly tripled between 1972 and 1975; real GDP/person rose about 23% in that interval. GDP is not personal income; nationalization alone does not establish causality.

**On-screen qualification:** GDP/person, 1972–75: nearly 3× in current US$. / Inflation-adjusted growth: about 23%.

**Optional revised narration:** Between 1972 and 1975, nominal GDP per person nearly tripled.

**Evidence:** S3, S4.

### education

**Supplied claim:** Every child received free education through university.

**Verdict:** policy_real_universal_outcome_unsubstantiated. State education was tuition-free through higher education. That does not mean every child attended university; postprimary study was optional.

**On-screen qualification:** Education was tuition-free through university. / Attendance and access were not universal.

**Optional revised narration:** State education was free through university, though access remained unequal.

**Evidence:** S2, S5.

### health

**Supplied claim:** Every citizen received free healthcare.

**Verdict:** policy_real_universal_outcome_unsubstantiated. A free public healthcare system existed, but entitlement is not proof every citizen actually received every needed service.

**On-screen qualification:** Public healthcare was provided free of charge. / This did not guarantee care reached everyone.

**Optional revised narration:** Public healthcare was free, supported by expanding hospitals and clinics.

**Evidence:** S6.

### unesco

**Supplied claim:** UNESCO awarded Iraq its literacy prize.

**Verdict:** supported_with_specificity. 1979 Krupskaya award recognizes the national literacy campaign, not proof illiteracy disappeared. State report explicitly records award.

**On-screen qualification:** 1979 · UNESCO Krupskaya literacy prize / Awarded for Iraq’s national literacy campaign.

**Optional revised narration:** In 1979, UNESCO’s Krupskaya prize recognized Iraq’s literacy campaign.

**Evidence:** S2.

### arab_reaction

**On-screen qualification:** The narration voices a pan-Arab interpretation. / Regional governments responded differently.

**Optional revised narration:** Supporters across the Arab world saw a declaration of sovereignty.

**Evidence:** S1, S12.

## Historical context required for the exact-wording edition

Development coexisted with dictatorship. The 1988 Anfal campaign targeted Kurdish civilians.

Human Rights Watch’s investigation drew on captured government records and survivor interviews. Its finding of genocide contradicts any implication that the period’s social programs established general freedom or the equal treatment of all Iraqi communities. Development and political repression occurred together.

## Data calculation

Current-dollar GDP/person ratio, 1975 ÷ 1972: 1,165.88163945653 ÷ 394.41834302923 = 2.95595. Constant-dollar ratio: 1,633.76336398088 ÷ 1,325.75580274961 = 1.23233. Thus a nominal near-tripling can be stated for those dates; a real tripling or tripling of everyone’s personal income cannot. The large nominal change also reflects the oil-price environment and is not causal proof of nationalization alone.

## Opening card qualifications

The opening text cards are also assertions and need contextual qualifications, either as legible small text on those cards or an immediately following factual caption. In particular, the zero-sharing statement is false if read literally: Iraq already received oil income. Nationalization changed ownership and control.

- **They drew your borders without asking.** Colonial powers shaped borders, but modern Iraq’s boundaries were settled through multiple commissions, negotiations and conflicts; the line is not a literal summary of a single agreement. Qualification: Britain and France shaped borders under mandate. / Boundaries emerged through several later agreements. Sources: S15, S16.
- **They took your oil without sharing.** Iraq received oil royalties and revenue before nationalization; nationalization changed ownership and control. Qualification: Iraq already received oil revenues. / Nationalization changed ownership and control. Sources: S1.
- **One party said enough.** Oil concessions were curtailed by Law 80 in 1961, before the 1968 Baath takeover. Nationalization in 1972 continued an older struggle. Qualification: Law 80 restricted concessions in 1961, / before the Ba’ath Party took lasting power in 1968. Sources: S1.

- **S14: [Recent changes in agrarian reform and rural development strategies in the Near East](https://www.fao.org/4/W4760E/w4760e0q.htm)** — M. Riad El-Ghonemy, FAO Land Reform. Full page read. Records Iraq reform dates 1958 and 1971; documents incomplete redistribution and subsequent military spending.

- **S15: [Report of the Commission Entrusted by the Council with the Study of the Frontier between Syria and Iraq](https://www.loc.gov/resource/g7461f.ct000889b/)** — Library of Congress, League of Nations historical item.
