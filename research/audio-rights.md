# Audio sources and processing

All score recordings are modern soundtrack material, never claimed to be Iraqi archival sound. No commercial recording is copied without a free reuse license. Machine-readable provenance, original URLs, license URLs, local paths and SHA-256 hashes are in `research/audio-assets.json`; source metadata evidence is in `research/audio-license-evidence.json`.

Excerpts 0–16 seconds and 46–63 seconds; fades and gain changes. This is a genuine oud performance. Wikimedia metadata records its commission and author.

Plucked strings, bowed strings and percussion; excerpts 0–90 seconds and 140–180 seconds crossfade beneath speech. The music is Arab-inspired production music, not an Iraqi historical ensemble recording.

[Source](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Oud_open_Strings.ogg), [CC BY-SA 3.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/3.0/). First open-string note, source 0.23–1.23 seconds; excerpted and faded at timeline 158–159 seconds. A note has no separately protected musical composition.

The rendered film adapts CC BY-SA 3.0 audio; publish the film under **CC BY-SA 4.0**, a later-version adaptation license allowed by CC BY-SA 3.0, with the attribution file accompanying the MP4. Do not strip credits when sharing the download. Separate archival sources retain their own underlying terms.

The similarly named Commons file `OUD.ogg` by Houssem bettaibi was rejected: although its recording is CC BY-SA 3.0, it performs part of “Hobb Eih,” composed by Baligh Hamdi, with a separate composition-rights problem. # Voice

The revised film uses **Kokoro-82M / Michael (`am_michael`)**, American English male, selected by the user after two auditions. The model and supplied English voice tensors are distributed under Apache-2.0; no separate English voice or audio-output restrictions are stated. See [primary rights evidence](kokoro-rights.md), [publisher](https://huggingface.co/hexgrad/Kokoro-82M), and `kokoro-license-evidence.json`. The `kokoro-onnx==0.4.9` runtime wrapper is MIT. Model and voice-pack bytes are SHA-256 pinned in the audio manifest.

The engine synthesizes full passages at natural speaking speed (1.0 main, 0.9 closing). Timestamped recognition checks the spoken wording and locates quiet gaps for individual cue boundaries. The pipeline preserves every source sample when splitting passages; subsequent scheduling changes only external silence. No pitch-shifting or audio time stretching is applied. Closing narration is quieter. ASR results and pronunciation handling are recorded in `kokoro-asr-qa.json`; automated recognition is not a human listening certification.

The production cache at `assets/audio/kokoro-segments/` contains the verified, generated project narration plus its source passages and provenance stamps. These are modern synthesized speech, not archival recordings. Recorded output is distributed as project material under CC BY-SA 4.0. The manifest records hashes; synthesis settings and source hashes must match before cached audio is reused. Changed text, voice or model triggers regeneration. The original Piper/Norman voice has been retired; its original rights records remain in `retired_voice_assets` for the first export's history.

# Upload alternative

Upload `assets/audio/uploaded-narration.wav` through GitHub's web interface (or enter an HTTPS narration URL in the workflow). Also upload `config/uploaded-transcript.txt`, containing the exact requested narration, and `config/uploaded-timing.json`, a list with one `{id, source_start, source_end}` per sentence. Copy `config/upload-timing-example.json` as a schema example, replacing the sample source times with the times in your recording. The pipeline rearranges only silence between the supplied sentence segments; it never changes speech tempo or pitch. Also provide `config/uploaded-audio-rights.json` with `creator`, `source_url`, `license`, `license_url`, `rights_confirmed: true`, and the original recording’s `sha256`. Permitted compatible license values are `CC0 1.0`, `CC BY 4.0`, `CC BY-SA 4.0`, and `Public domain`. The uploader must check that the audio actually matches the supplied transcript and owns or licenses the recording. Rights are checked before downloading an override, and its actual hash is checked before use. Missing or mismatching companions stop the job instead of silently producing unsynchronized audio.

# Verification limits

FFprobe verifies that the completed mix is 160.000000 seconds, 48 kHz stereo PCM before final AAC encoding. Music is ducked from the actual voice signal, with peak limiting. Automated ASR, when available, is a spot check; it cannot replace human listening for naturalness, pronunciation or editorial quality. No human listening approval is claimed by these scripts.
