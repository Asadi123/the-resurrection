# Audio sources and processing

All score recordings are modern soundtrack material, never claimed to be Iraqi archival sound. No commercial recording is copied without a free reuse license. Machine-readable provenance, original URLs, license URLs, local paths and SHA-256 hashes are in `research/audio-assets.json`; source metadata evidence is in `research/audio-license-evidence.json`.

* **Opening and closing oud:** “Oud music by Andy R. Jordan 1V2 long,” Andy R. Jordan, commissioned by the Wikimedia Foundation, 12 April 2018. [Original description](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Oud_music_by_Andy_R._Jordan_1V2_long.mp3), [CC BY-SA 3.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/3.0/). Excerpts 0–16 seconds and 46–63 seconds; fades and gain changes. This is a genuine oud performance. Wikimedia metadata records its commission and author.
* **Arabic-inspired orchestral middle and percussion peak:** “Ibn Al-Noor,” Kevin MacLeod (incompetech.com), uploaded 26 May 2010, ISRC USUAN1100706. [Composer/record-producer source and license](https://incompetech.com/music/royalty-free/index.html?Search=Search&isrc=USUAN1100706), [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). Plucked strings, bowed strings and percussion; excerpts 0–90 seconds and 140–180 seconds crossfade beneath speech. The music is Arab-inspired production music, not an Iraqi historical ensemble recording.
* **Final single oud note:** “Oud open Strings,” Bottomline, original upload 13 February 2006; Ogg conversion by Ilmari Karonen, 20 June 2010. [Source](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Oud_open_Strings.ogg), [CC BY-SA 3.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/3.0/). First open-string note, source 0.23–1.23 seconds; excerpted and faded at timeline 158–159 seconds. A note has no separately protected musical composition.

The rendered film adapts CC BY-SA 3.0 audio; publish the film under **CC BY-SA 4.0**, a later-version adaptation license allowed by CC BY-SA 3.0, with the attribution file accompanying the MP4. Do not strip credits when sharing the download. Separate archival sources retain their own underlying terms.

The similarly named Commons file `OUD.ogg` by Houssem bettaibi was rejected: although its recording is CC BY-SA 3.0, it performs part of “Hobb Eih,” composed by Baligh Hamdi, with a separate composition-rights problem. Piper `lessac`, `joe`, and `ryan` were also rejected because of research-only or noncommercial dataset lineage.

# Voice

**Norman**, US English male, synthesized locally with free open-source `piper-tts==1.3.0`. [Original model card](https://huggingface.co/rhasspy/piper-voices/blob/main/en/en_US/norman/medium/MODEL_CARD) identifies a male voice trained from scratch on public-domain LibriVox recordings. [Model creator Bryce Beattie](https://brycebeattie.com/files/tts/) explicitly labels Norman public domain and permits reuse. The upstream voice repository metadata is MIT; retain its notice when redistributing the model. Piper 1.3 runtime source is [OHF-Voice/piper1-gpl](https://github.com/OHF-Voice/piper1-gpl), GPL-3.0; runtime licensing does not make synthesized speech GPL. The model is downloaded and hash-verified, not committed as a large repository blob.

This voice is modern synthetic narration, not archival testimony or an imitation of a named historical person. The exact user-provided text is preserved in `config/narration.json` and `config/narration-transcript.txt`. Publication still depends on the project's separate factual-context review. Synthesis duration settings make the closing slower; no rendered audio is accelerated, stretched or pitch-shifted. Actual per-sentence timings are measured after synthesis and saved in `output/audio-timing.json`, with a 1.2-second reflective pause before the closing. Explicit phonemes `/bɑːθɪzəm/` correct eSpeak's handling of the curly apostrophe in Ba’athism; date digits are spoken equivalently as “June first, nineteen seventy two.” These pronunciation settings are reproducible and do not change the transcript's words or meaning.

# Upload alternative

Upload `assets/audio/uploaded-narration.wav` through GitHub's web interface (or enter an HTTPS narration URL in the workflow). Also upload `config/uploaded-transcript.txt`, containing the exact requested narration, and `config/uploaded-timing.json`, a list with one `{id, source_start, source_end}` per sentence. Copy `config/upload-timing-example.json` as a schema example, replacing the sample source times with the times in your recording. The pipeline rearranges only silence between the supplied sentence segments; it never changes speech tempo or pitch. The uploader must check that the audio actually matches the supplied transcript and owns or licenses the recording. Missing or mismatching companions stop the job instead of silently producing unsynchronized audio.

# Verification limits

FFprobe verifies that the completed mix is 160.000000 seconds, 48 kHz stereo PCM before final AAC encoding. Music is ducked from the actual voice signal, with peak limiting. Automated ASR, when available, is a spot check; it cannot replace human listening for naturalness, pronunciation or editorial quality. No human listening approval is claimed by these scripts.
